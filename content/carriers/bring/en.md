Bring is the parcel brand of Posten Bring, Norway's state-owned postal group; Posten is its brand for people in Norway. Both share one tracking: use sporing.bring.no, sporing.posten.no or the Posten app, or paste the number in the box above. The number comes from the shop, or from Bring or Posten by SMS or email.

## Bring tracking number format

Bring says an item or parcel number generally has 13 characters (two letters, nine digits, two letters), or is a series of 18 digits starting with 3 or 17 digits starting with 7. Enter the whole combination.

| Number (invented) | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `RR000000005NO` | Two letters, nine digits, `NO`: an item number. |
| `370001111000000006` | 18 digits starting with `3`. |
| `00370000000000000004` | `00` and an 18-digit number starting `370`: 20 digits in all. |
| `70000000000000003` | 17 digits starting with `7`. |

**Where to find it.** In the shop's shipping email, in an SMS or email from Bring or Posten, or as a barcode on Posten's mailbox notice. Bring's own tracking also accepts the sender's reference or your mobile number.

**Sendingsnummer or kollinummer?** A shipment (sending) can hold several packages (kolli): the sendingsnummer covers the whole shipment, each kollinummer one package, and Bring's tracking lists every package. Either is a sporingsnummer (tracking number), and Bring's tracking takes both.

**No result?** Check that the parcel went with Posten or Bring, and that you didn't enter an order number. Lookalikes: a postal number ending `SE` or `DK` is [PostNord's](carrier:postnord), one ending `FI` belongs to Finland's [Posti](carrier:posti).

## What Bring's tracking statuses mean

Bring's tracking opens in Norwegian on sporing.posten.no and sporing.bring.no, with a switch to English; tracking.bring.se and tracking.bring.dk show Swedish and Danish. There's no Finnish. The English wordings, in the order a parcel meets them:

| Bring says | What it means for you |
| --- | --- |
| `The sender has let us know that a parcel is on its way.` | The shop has booked the delivery. Bring doesn't have the parcel yet. |
| `The shipment has been handed in to Bring.` | Bring has the parcel. |
| `The shipment was delivered after the deadline and will be sent the next working day.` | Handed in after the day's cut-off. It leaves the next working day; nothing is wrong. |
| `The shipment has arrived at the terminal.` | At one of Bring's sorting terminals. |
| `The shipment being processed for import.` | Going through customs (Bring's grammar). |
| `The shipment has been temporarily placed in warehouse.` | Waiting at the local depot for the delivery run. |
| `The shipment is  unfortunately delayed and will be forwarded by first possible dispatch.` | Delayed, and leaves with the next transport. |
| `The parcel is now on its way to the delivery address.` | Out for delivery (utkjøring): loaded on the delivery vehicle. |
| `The parcel is ready for pickup` | Waiting for you at the pickup point named in the tracking. |
| `The parcel has been delivered in your mailbox or outside your door.` | Delivered. |
| `The shipment has been returned.` | On its way back to the sender, for example after the pickup deadline. |

`The shipment has been temporarily placed in warehouse.` sounds like storage, but the Norwegian original means the depot that sends out the delivery vans. Lines such as `Home delivery date is changed.` or `The recipient has selected a desired delivery location.` record a change to the delivery; the parcel hasn't moved.

**Gaps are normal.** Bring and Posten say there's no tracking between terminals, which can take 1 to 4 days. Once it's registered at your local terminal, it's normally out for delivery the same day, or at your pickup point the next day. See [tracking that isn't updating](guide:tracking-not-updating).

## Bring pickup point, parcel locker and home delivery

These rules are for Norway; in Sweden and Denmark, check bring.se or bring.dk.

**Where it waits.** At a post office, a Post i Butikk (post in a shop) or a Pakkeboks parcel locker, as the tracking usually says; terminals aren't pickup points. Lockers take parcels up to 10 kg. You're told by the Posten app, SMS or email, with up to two reminders.

**How long it waits.** Posten's current deadlines:

- Pakkeboks locker: 4 days, extendable free of charge to 7.
- Other pickup points: 7 days, extendable free of charge to 14 in the Posten app or by SMS, for a limited period.
- Parcels with only a paper notice, such as some from abroad: 14 days.

Some senders allow no extension, and the tracking page shows each parcel's own deadline. An uncollected parcel goes back to the sender. Take ID: some parcels need it.

**Home delivery.** If you chose it in the shop, Posten delivers on the weekday you pick, 17:00 to 21:00 in towns (15:00 to 22:00 around Oslo, Stokke and Drammen) and during the day elsewhere; for an evening delivery, an SMS gives a one-hour window. To move a pickup-point parcel to your door, use the link in the message: NOK 75 before it reaches the pickup point, NOK 100 after; not for locker or customs parcels.

**Mailbox parcels.** A small parcel sent as Pakke i postkassen comes with the post. If it won't fit, it's left outside your door (mark the door with your name), or goes to your pickup point if you choose that when Posten tells you it's coming; from there it can't be redirected home.

**Big items.** Bring's Home Delivery carries goods over 35 kg, such as furniture or white goods, at the level chosen in the shop: Curbside (to the kerb or plot boundary), Indoor (carried in to the spot you point out) or PickUp Point (you fetch it from a Home Delivery terminal). Without a booked slot, Bring texts you to agree a day and time window; the driver contacts you before arriving, and you normally sign for it.

**Sweden and Denmark.** In Sweden, a parcel normally waits 7 days at an ombud (agent), and the first reminder offers 7 more; in a paketbox (locker) it waits 4 days, extendable to 7. A paketbox opens with the Bring app or the PIN Bring sends by SMS. In Denmark, a missed home delivery normally goes to the nearest pakkeshop (pickup point), and Bring tells you by SMS or email; if not, book a redelivery with Bring's online form or by phone.

## Bring parcel delayed, lost or damaged

**Not moving.** If nothing updates in the expected time, Bring asks you to contact the sender, who can check whether the parcel was handed in.

**Damaged or missing items.** Bring says a claim must come from the sender or whoever paid for transport; as the recipient, tell the shop as soon as possible. Bring's deadlines: visible damage or missing items at once, on receipt; hidden damage within 7 calendar days; other issues such as delay within 14 days of delivery. Every claim expires after a year. Take photos and keep the goods. Marked delivered but not there? See [delivered but not received](guide:delivered-not-received).

**Customs in Norway.** From shops in Norway's VOEC scheme (items under NOK 3,000), nothing is due on arrival. Otherwise Posten charges NOK 46 to clear a parcel worth up to NOK 500, NOK 78 up to 3,000, NOK 278 above. Pay in the Posten app, Digipost or by letter. More in [parcels held at customs](guide:customs).

## Tracking Bring parcels with Peek

[Peek](/) names a postal number ending `NO` as Bring's straight away when its check digit is valid. The 18-digit `370`/`373` numbers, the 20-digit barcodes starting `00370` or `00373` and 17-digit numbers starting `70` are shared with other carriers, so Peek asks Bring first, together with the other carriers the number fits ([Matkahuolto](carrier:matkahuolto) too for `373`), and names Bring whenever Bring knows the parcel. If Bring doesn't come up, tap "Change" and pick Bring, listed first under "Fits this number". The exception is an 18-digit number starting `370` or `373` whose 6th to 9th digits are zeros: Peek files it under [Ecoscooting](carrier:ecoscooting) without asking Bring, so tap "Change" and search for Bring. Links from sporing.bring.no, sporing.posten.no and tracking.bring.com, .se or .dk name Bring whatever the number, but at Bring Peek can only track 17- or 18-digit numbers, 20-digit ones starting `00`, and postal numbers (two letters, nine digits, two letters) with a valid check digit, from any country. A sendingsnummer for several parcels isn't tracked; use each kollinummer.

Peek shows the status, each scan's town on a map, the sender, service, weight, dimensions, the expected day when Bring gives one and, while the parcel waits, the pickup point's name. Instead of Bring's sentences, each step gets a short label such as "Handed in", "At terminal" or "Ready for pickup". It drops addresses, photos and the pickup code, checks every 10 minutes by day (every 2 once out for delivery) and every hour or two overnight, and can notify you of changes.

## Questions about Bring tracking

### Is Bring the same as Posten?

Not quite: both are brands of Posten Bring, with one shared tracking. Posten serves people in Norway, Bring businesses in the Nordics and private customers outside Norway. Businesses track their shipments in Mybring, Bring's logged-in portal, up to 50 numbers at a time.

### How long does Posten take to deliver?

Posten says Norgespakke and pickup parcels usually take 2 to 5 business days in Norway. Bring Denmark delivers most parcels within 1 to 2 days.

### What time of day do Posten and Bring deliver?

The post comes between 08:00 and 17:00, at a time that varies a little from day to day, with letters every other weekday. Parcels to your door come on weekdays, 17:00 to 21:00 in towns and during the day elsewhere.

### How long does Bring keep a parcel in a locker?

In Norway, 4 days in a Pakkeboks, which you can extend free of charge to 7. Then it goes back to the sender.

### What happens if a Bring parcel goes missing?

Contact the shop: the sender files the claim with Bring, and all claims expire after a year.
