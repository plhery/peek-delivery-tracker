GLS Germany delivers on weekdays and, by its group's count, has 9,000 ParcelShops and 200 Parcelstations in Germany. Track a parcel on [GLS's tracking page](https://gls-group.com/EU/en/parcel-tracking) with its parcel number and the recipient's postcode, or paste the number in the box above. The number is in the shop's email, and GLS's notification card carries a Track-ID.

## GLS tracking number and Track-ID: where to find them

Check the shop's shipping email first. Tracking works once the sender has sent GLS the parcel's data, and a parcel a shop abroad sends with GLS is tracked on the same GLS group page. GLS's notification card, on paper or by email, carries a Track-ID of six letters, which works like the parcel number in GLS's tracking.

Peek knows these shapes:

| Number (invented) | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `11111111111` | 11 digits. Also fits [GLS Switzerland](carrier:gls-ch), [GLS France](carrier:gls-fr), GLS Austria, GLS Poland, PostLogistics and others. |
| `123459999981` | 12 digits, the last a check digit. Also fits [BRT](carrier:brt), [Mondial Relay](carrier:mondial-relay), Colis Privé and others. |
| `AB123456` | 8 letters and digits: a Track-ID can look like this too. Also fits GLS Switzerland, GLS France and Loggi. |
| Track-ID | Six letters, on the notification card. Peek reads it like a parcel number; if it doesn't take yours, use the parcel number. |

Number refused? If the shop's email names another carrier, the number is theirs: see [tracking number formats](guide:tracking-number-formats).

## What GLS tracking statuses mean

GLS's English wordings, in the order a parcel meets them:

| GLS says | What it means for you |
| --- | --- |
| `The parcel data was entered into the GLS IT system…`, `The parcel has not been handed over to GLS.` | GLS has the sender's data, not yet the parcel. |
| `The parcel was handed over to GLS.` | GLS has it. |
| `The parcel has not been released by customs.`, `Customs Consignment via Customs Portal` | A parcel from outside the EU is still held at customs; Peek shows "At customs". |
| `The parcel was released by customs.` | Customs has let it go and it's on its way again; Peek shows "In transit". |
| `The parcel is in delivery.`, `The parcel is expected to be delivered during the day.` | In the GLS van, expected today. |
| `The parcel is ready for collection at the ParcelShop.` | Waiting for you at a ParcelShop or Parcelstation. |
| `The parcel has been delivered.` | Delivered to you or to someone who took it in for you, such as a neighbour. |

In between, scans say when the parcel reaches or leaves a GLS parcel centre or depot: it's on its way. If the driver missed you, or delivery wasn't possible for another reason, Peek shows "Delivery attempted". GLS then tries once more or takes the parcel to a nearby ParcelShop, depot or Parcelstation, and tells you by card or email where it is. If you don't collect it, or it can't be delivered at all, it goes back to the sender and Peek shows "Returned to sender". GLS counts a ParcelShop arrival as delivered, though the parcel is still waiting for you there. `The parcel has not been handed over to GLS.` stays the latest step until the sender hands the parcel over, so ask the sender.

## GLS delivery times, ParcelShops and parcel lockers

**When.** Monday to Friday, never Saturday, within estimated delivery times that GLS doesn't guarantee.

**Letterbox.** A small parcel, up to 2 kg, no larger than DIN C4 and no more than 5 cm high, may go straight into your letterbox.

**Nobody home.** The driver may hand the parcel to someone at your home or business, or to a neighbour in the same or the nearest building, unless you or the sender said otherwise; a card or email names the neighbour. Otherwise GLS asks you to collect it from the nearest ParcelShop, depot or Parcelstation. GLS makes at most two delivery attempts.

**Changing it.** Where GLS offers delivery options, you can pick another day or address, a ParcelShop or collection at the depot; GLS's consumer information from January 2018 says they're free for recipients. For a parcel from abroad sent with GLS's FlexDeliveryService, GLS keeps you posted, gives the expected day or time window where it can and offers changes; if you picked a ParcelShop at a foreign shop's checkout (ShopDeliveryService), the parcel goes straight there, with no attempt at your address. A drop-off permit lets the driver leave parcels at a place you named, and GLS tells you by card or email when it has.

**ParcelShops** keep a parcel left after a missed delivery at least seven working days, say GLS's terms for business senders. Uncollected parcels go back to the sender.

**Parcelstations** are GLS's lockers, and you need your own device to use one. You can object to GLS leaving a parcel in one, once or for good: the missed-delivery notice says how.

## GLS parcel not delivered, late or damaged

GLS's contract is with the sender, so refunds go through the shop.

**No update.** Ask the sender, and see [why tracking stops updating](guide:tracking-not-updating).

**Delivered but not there.** Check the tracking for a ParcelShop, and your letterbox and email for GLS's card naming a neighbour or drop-off place. More in [delivered but not received](guide:delivered-not-received).

**At customs.** A parcel from outside the EU has to clear customs. GLS says duty, import VAT and usually a customs clearance fee must be paid before the parcel can be delivered; who pays the clearance fee depends on the delivery terms the sender chose. In the EU, a flat €3 customs duty per item type has applied since 1 July 2026 to online orders worth up to €150. Since 1 November 2026, a €2 EU handling fee per item type applies too, whatever the value, owed by whoever declares the goods to customs. More in [parcels held at customs](guide:customs).

**Damaged.** German law (Commercial Code, § 438) wants visible damage noted at delivery (telling the driver will do), and hidden damage reported within 7 days and delay within 21, in text form; sending the notice in time is enough. Loss or damage can be reported by the recipient or the sender. A shop selling to you carries the transit risk unless you hired the carrier yourself and the shop hadn't named it to you (Civil Code, § 475), so ask the shop first.

**Disputes.** Loss or damage you can't settle with GLS can go to the Bundesnetzagentur's Schlichtungsstelle Post, whether you sent or received the parcel. It's free, for disputes worth €10 to €2,500, and GLS must take part when a consumer applies.

**Withdrawal (Widerruf).** Not collecting a parcel doesn't withdraw you from the purchase. If you bought as a consumer from a business at a distance, such as online, or away from its premises, you can usually withdraw by telling the business, clearly and without giving a reason, within 14 days (Civil Code, § 355). For goods, the period starts once you have received them (§ 356), and sending your notice in time is enough.

## GLS Germany customer service and contact

For a parcel from a shop, the shop is your first contact. GLS Germany's own contact options are on gls-pakete.de, its German site. GLS's group asks you to send questions about a parcel's status to your country's customer service, and says the hotline number is on that country's GLS site; the [group's contact form](https://gls-group.com/GROUP/en/contact-form/) also takes recipients' requests. If you sent a parcel yourself through GLS-Pakete.de or the app, its terms (August 2023) give versand-service@gls-germany.com and +49 6677 646907040.

> GLS's group warns of fake emails, SMS and websites in its name. Its main website is gls-group.com, and only that site and the sites it links to, such as gls-pakete.de, are genuine. GLS never asks recipients by email to pay for goods by card, transfer or online payment. For a parcel from outside the EU, though, GLS or an authorised agent may ask you by email, SMS or letter to pay duties and taxes. Cash-on-delivery parcels sent with the GLS Cash Service are handed over only against cash paid to the driver, and GLS advises against paying for a cash-on-delivery parcel you didn't expect, for yourself or for a neighbour. Send suspicious emails as an attachment to security@gls-group.com.

## Tracking a GLS Germany parcel with Peek

[Peek](/) asks the likely carriers about the number shapes above. GLS answers for German and Swiss parcels alike, and Peek names GLS Germany ("GLS Germany has this parcel"), or GLS France if it knows the parcel. In Switzerland or Liechtenstein (by your iPhone's region or web connection) it names GLS Switzerland, with a 4-digit postcode field: for a parcel to Germany, press "Change" and pick GLS Germany. A pasted gls-pakete.de link, or a gls-group one with /DE/, /EU/ or /GROUP/, also names GLS Germany.

GLS needs the delivery postcode for the detailed history, so the "Delivery postcode" field is required: without it Peek only learns whether GLS knows the number, and if GLS doesn't find the parcel with the one you gave, Peek says so and lets you change it. Any country's format works. It goes to GLS and, when needed, to tracking services.

You then see the status, each scan's town and country on a map, the expected day, the weight, the delivery time and, at a ParcelShop, its name and address. Your name, address and contact details, the signature and the sender's reference are dropped. Peek shows GLS's ParcelShop step as "Ready for pickup". Times are read as German time. Peek checks GLS about once an hour, delivery day included (4 hours after a failed check), and refreshing by hand doesn't make it check sooner. Only a parcel GLS hands to Swiss Post is followed to another carrier. When another carrier takes over the delivery, Peek follows the pace of the carrier delivering the parcel. Peek can notify you of each change.

## Questions about GLS tracking in Germany

### Where is my GLS package?

GLS's tracking shows it once you enter the number and the recipient's postcode: `The parcel is in delivery.` means it's in the van today.

### What is the GLS Track-ID?

The six-letter code on GLS's notification card. It works like the parcel number in GLS's tracking.

### How long does GLS take to deliver?

GLS lists estimated delivery times on gls-pakete.de and doesn't guarantee them. Its group says a parcel within one country usually arrives in under 24 hours, and one sent across Europe in 24 to 96 hours. Only a parcel the sender booked with GLS's Guaranteed24Service is due the next working day (Monday to Friday) within Germany, except on its islands.

### How long does GLS keep a parcel?

After a missed delivery, the ParcelShop, depot or Parcelstation keeps it at least seven working days. Then it goes back to the sender.

### Is GLS legit?

Yes: GLS Germany is General Logistics Systems Germany GmbH & Co. OHG, of Neuenstein. Fake messages use its name, though: see the warning above.
