A text or email saying your parcel is on hold, needs a small fee or a corrected address, with a link to sort it out, is almost always a scam. Don't tap anything: open the carrier's website or app yourself and track the number from the shop's own shipping email. If you already typed in card details, call your bank now on the number on the back of your card (in the UK, dial 159).

## How do I know if a delivery text is genuine?

A fake usually shows several of these:

- **You never asked for updates.** USPS only texts if you requested tracking yourself, and [Canada Post](carrier:canada-post) only if you signed up.
- **It wants money to deliver.** A redelivery fee, "unpaid shipping fees", a small charge to release the parcel. DPD UK says it "will never ask you to pay a 'redelivery fee' via a link", and Evri never asks for payment to reschedule a delivery.
- **It hurries you.** Fakes set a deadline, such as the "within 48 hours" in one that Australia Post quotes. ComReg, Ireland's communications regulator, says not to rush to answer texts that seem urgent.
- **The link isn't the carrier's own.** It can take you to a spoofed website that looks real but isn't, the FTC warns. The address gives it away, as shown below.
- **The sender is a mobile number or an email address.** Evri texts only ever come from "Evri", and DPD UK's usually show "DPD UK", "DPD Local" or "DPDgroup UK". DPD UK says a random number or an address like random123@icloud.com in their place means a scam.
- **It asks you to reply "Y", call a number or install an app,** or greets you as "Dear Customer", which DPD UK says it never writes.

A familiar sender name proves nothing: [Australia Post](carrier:australia-post) warns that scammers can set it so a fake lands in the same thread as its real messages.

Since 1 July 2026, Australian phones show texts from unregistered sender names as `Unverified`. In Ireland, they're labelled `Likely Scam`, but ComReg says genuine businesses that haven't registered can get that label too. Treat both labels as a reason to check, not as a verdict.

### How to read a link before you tap it

Scam links often start with the carrier's name. What counts is the end of the address, just before the first single `/`. That part names the owner, as in this made-up example:

:::anatomy royalmail.co.uk. redelivery-fee.example /pay
- royalmail.co.uk. | The carrier's name, put first as bait. Here it's only a label
- redelivery-fee.example | The real owner of the address: not Royal Mail
- /pay | The page that asks for your card
:::

Even if a link looks right, type the carrier's address yourself.

## Do USPS, Evri, DPD and other carriers send texts?

Yes, most do, which is why fakes work. Here's what each says it sends, and what it never does:

| Carrier | When it texts or emails you | What it says it never does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [USPS](carrier:usps) | Only if you asked for tracking; Text Tracking uses short code 28777 | Put a link in those texts, or charge for the service |
| [Evri](carrier:evri) | Texts from "Evri" with a link to evri.com only | Ask you to reschedule or pay, or to install an app |
| [DPD UK](carrier:dpd-uk) | Texts usually from "DPD UK", "DPD Local" or "DPDgroup UK" | Ask for a redelivery fee through a link |
| Canada Post | Short codes 272727 or 55555, only if you signed up | Ask for card details or payment by text or email |
| Australia Post | Texts from its registered sender name | Text, email or call to ask for payment or card details |

Real carriers do send links, so a link alone proves nothing either way. The few real payment requests are nearly all for import charges: in the UK, GOV.UK says Royal Mail, Parcelforce or the courier will contact you with a bill stating exactly which fees you need to pay. The [customs guide](guide:customs) shows how carriers really ask for them.

## What do fake delivery texts say?

Fakes recycle a few scripts. These are quoted from carriers' warning pages:

- **Missed delivery, pay to rebook.** A fake "missed parcel" message, often badly worded: "is missed delivery" (Evri).
- **Address problem.** "Shipment has been suspended due to missing house number", "Update your correct address", "verify your postcode within 48 hours" (Australia Post).
- **Parcel on hold.** "On hold due to unpaid shipping fees", with a link to pay (Canada Post).
- **"Reply Y."** Evri and Australia Post explain the trick: replying makes your phone trust the sender, so the link becomes clickable.
- **iMessage instead of SMS.** DPD UK says recent fakes arrive mainly as iMessages, while its real alerts are plain SMS.
- **A phone call.** Australia Post never calls out of the blue to ask for payment.
- **A courier for your marketplace sale.** Australia Post never asks you to click a social media message to book a courier for something you're selling.

## How to check a parcel text safely

None of these steps uses the message:

:::steps
- Are you expecting anything? | No order, and nobody who would post you something? Treat it as a scam.
- Find the real tracking number | Use the shop's shipping email or order page, never the number in the text.
- Open the carrier yourself | Type its address or open its app. Don't tap, scan or call anything in the message.
- Look for a real request | A fee that's really due should show on the carrier's own site or app.
- Report it, then delete it | Forward texts to 7726 in the UK, US and Canada.
:::

Canada Post says its own tracking is the safest way to see whether any fee is due. Can't find the number? See [where to find your tracking number](guide:find-tracking-number). Then you can paste it into [Peek](/), which asks the carrier and, when needed, other sources, so you see the latest scans without going near the link.

A real missed delivery usually leaves a card. Canada Post leaves a delivery notice card and says an email asking you to click a link after a missed delivery "is NOT from Canada Post". For genuine cards and rebooking, see [missed delivery](guide:missed-delivery).

## Clicked the link in a scam text? What to do now

A scam text does no harm until you tap, reply, call or type something in. What to do next depends on how far you went:

:::steps
- Close the page | Enter nothing more. Don't reply, even "STOP".
- Call your bank or card issuer | Use the number on your card or banking app, and ask to block the card.
- Change your passwords | Wherever you typed or reused the one you entered.
- Say no to callers | Someone calls next, even as your bank? Hang up and call back on a number you found yourself, as Ireland's Garda advises.
- Report it | To the police or a fraud service if you lost money or details (see the table below).
:::

**Only tapped the link?** Report the text and tell your bank anyway. The U.S. Postal Inspection Service advises this once you've interacted with the page, "even if you did not click 'submit'".

**Entered card details or paid the fee?** Call your bank at once to stop the card. In the UK, 159 connects you to most banks, and Stop Scams UK says it "cannot be spoofed". In the US, the FTC says to tell your card issuer at once and ask for a refund; federal law protects you from unauthorised use of your credit card. Then watch your statements: [DPD Germany](carrier:dpd-de) warns that these card forms can sign you up for further payments, so even a tiny fee isn't harmless.

## Got a scam or spam text? Where to report it

Report a fake delivery text even if you didn't click, then delete it.

| Country | Report the message | Lost money or details |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UK | Forward texts to 7726 (free); emails to report@phishing.gov.uk | Report Fraud, reportfraud.police.uk or 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales, Northern Ireland); Police Scotland on 101 |
| US | Forward texts to 7726; USPS fakes to spam@uspis.gov with a screenshot; ReportFraud.ftc.gov | Your bank; the FBI's IC3 at ic3.gov |
| Ireland | Mark it as spam and block the sender, says ComReg; you can also report a suspected scam to your local Garda station or garda.ie | Your bank, then your local Garda station |
| Canada | Forward texts to 7726 | Your local police and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre, online or 1-888-495-8501 |
| Australia | Report junk in your messaging app; Australia Post fakes to scams@auspost.com.au; Scamwatch | Your bank, then report a cybercrime at cyber.gov.au; iDCare on 1800 595 160 for support |

On an iPhone, an iMessage from someone not in your contacts shows **Report Junk** under it; in Google Messages on Android, use **Block and report spam**.
