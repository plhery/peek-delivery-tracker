"Returned to sender" means the carrier couldn't hand your parcel (or package) over and is taking it back to whoever sent it, because of the address, a refusal, a missed pickup deadline or unpaid charges. Once a return has started, carriers such as [Canada Post](carrier:canada-post) and DHL say they can't stop it. Message the seller straight away and say, in writing, whether you want it sent again or your money back.

Sent something back to a shop yourself? That's a different journey: [tracking a return and your refund](guide:returns-tracking).

## Why was my parcel returned to sender?

The reason is usually in the latest scan, and the carrier marks it on the parcel for the sender.

| Reason | What the carrier says |
| --- | --- |
| Address wrong or incomplete | [USPS](carrier:usps) `Insufficient Address`, `No Such Number`, `No Such Street`; [DHL](carrier:dhl): no clear name on the doorbell or letterbox |
| Nobody known at the address, or moved | USPS `Moved, Left No Address`, `Vacant`; Canada Post `Recipient not located at address provided. Item being returned to sender.` |
| Not collected in time | USPS `Unclaimed`; [An Post](carrier:an-post) `Your item was not collected within the specified timeframe. This item will now be returned to sender.` |
| Refused at the door | USPS `Refused`; [Australia Post](carrier:australia-post): "someone declined delivery" |
| Customs charges unpaid or paperwork incomplete | An Post: charges not paid by the "pay by date", or the sender's customs declaration incomplete |
| Postage or label problem | Canada Post: not enough postage, an improper label, a reused tracking number |
| Not allowed in the post | USPS `Returned to Sender, Mailpiece Contains Nonmailable Contents` |
| The sender asked for it back | [Royal Mail](carrier:royal-mail) tells you when a business sender has used Stop and Return |

Post sent abroad often comes back with the reason in French, as the Universal Postal Union's rules have it: `Inconnu` (unknown), `Refusé` (refused), `Non réclamé` (unclaimed).

Some statuses hide a return, such as Australia Post's `Contact sender` and Royal Mail's `Forwarded to Returns Centre`: [tracking statuses](guide:tracking-statuses) explains them. If the reason was a customs charge, [parcels held at customs](guide:customs) covers what was owed and how to claim duties back.

## Can I stop my parcel from being returned to sender?

Once it has turned back, no. Before that, usually only the sender can. "Once the return process starts, we can't stop it," says Canada Post. DHL says the same, even for a parcel you refused. [UPS](carrier:ups) can't retrieve a parcel that's back with the sender, and Royal Mail's Stop and Return can't touch an item already scanned as Return to Sender.

The stop services that exist belong to the sender, and some need a business account:

| Carrier | Service | Who can use it | Cost and limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| USPS | Package Intercept | The sender or their authorised agent, with a USPS.com account | $19.45 per piece, charged only if the parcel is found; US only; not once it's out for delivery |
| UPS | Delivery Intercept, `Return to Sender` | The shipper | Before delivery; "fees may apply", charged only if it works |
| Royal Mail | Stop and Return | Business accounts (Click & Drop, Online Business Account) | Tracked, Special Delivery and [Parcelforce](carrier:parcelforce) express; UK only; not once at a collection point; Tracked and Parcelforce charged at the outbound rate, Special Delivery already includes it |
| Australia Post | Recall or redirect | Business credit account holders | eParcel and Parcel Send to a street address; the return charge in the sender's contract |

So while the parcel is still on its way to you, the seller is the one to ask. If you only need it delivered somewhere else, see [change the delivery address](guide:change-delivery-address).

## What to do when your parcel is returned to sender

:::steps
- Read the reason | It's in the latest scan.
- Message the seller today | Usually only the sender can recall a parcel, and only before the return starts.
- Say what you want, in writing | Sent again, or a refund. Refusing alone doesn't cancel an order.
- Keep the evidence | Screenshots of the tracking, and every email.
- Wait for it to land | The shop refunds you or ships again once it's back.
:::

## Do I get a refund if my parcel is returned to sender?

Usually, once it's back with the seller. Marketplaces have their own rules:

- **Amazon** orders can't be cancelled once shipped, but you can refuse the delivery, says Amazon UK; Amazon Australia gives a refund or replacement for a refused parcel it fulfilled. For a third-party seller, contact the seller first: on Amazon UK, if you want a refund and it hasn't answered within 48 hours, most orders are covered by the A-to-z Guarantee, and you claim from Amazon directly.
- **eBay**'s Money Back Guarantee refunds an item that never reached your checkout address, postage included, but not if you gave a wrong address, refused it (unless the postage was underpaid) or didn't pay import charges.
- **Vinted** cancels the order once the parcel is on its way back and refunds "the item's price, including shipping and Vinted fees". If the seller doesn't mark it received within 14 business days, the refund starts on its own; still nothing after 25 business days, contact Vinted.

From an ordinary online shop, the law where you live decides:

| Where | Does refusing or not collecting cancel the order? | Refund |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UK | No: you must tell the shop, with any "clear statement" | Within 14 days of the shop getting the parcel back; delivery refunded up to its cheapest common option |
| Ireland | No: the CCPC says "You must inform the trader" | Within 14 days of your telling the shop, once it has the parcel back |
| EU | No: a clear statement is needed. France's Service-Public says refusing delivery isn't enough | Within 14 days of your message, but the shop may wait until the parcel is back |
| Switzerland | No legal right to cancel an online purchase | The shop's terms |
| US | No general right: the FTC's Cooling-Off Rule leaves out online sales | The shop's policy |
| Canada | Consumer law is provincial | Start with the shop's policy |
| Australia | No remedy for a change of mind, says the ACCC | A shop must follow its own change-of-mind policy |

> Refusing a parcel or leaving it at the counter isn't cancelling. In the UK and EU, email the shop or use its cancellation form and keep a copy: that message is what obliges the shop to refund you.

## Who pays for the return?

The sender pays the carrier, and how much depends on the service:

- **USPS** returns Priority Mail Express, Priority Mail, First-Class Mail and Ground Advantage "at no additional charge".
- **Canada Post** charges the sender for an uncollected parcel's return; for international returns, usually the original postage again.
- **Royal Mail** sends it back by the same service it was sent with.
- **Australia Post** charges business senders the return fee in their contract.

In the UK and EU, the shop can pass that cost on to you only as the "direct cost of returning the goods", if it told you before you ordered and once you've cancelled. It may also keep the extra you paid for faster delivery.

## What happens when a parcel is returned to sender

The parcel goes back along much the same route, and the tracking runs backwards:

:::journey
- shop | Seller | Sends the parcel to you.
- warehouse | Hubs | Scans head towards you.
- locker | Near you | Refused, not collected or no one at the address.
- truck | Turns back | A line such as `Returning to sender`.
- warehouse | Hubs again | The same route, in reverse.
- shop | Seller | "Delivered" here means delivered to the sender.
:::

[Austrian Post](carrier:austrian-post) says a "delivered" on a return means delivered to the sender, and Australia Post shows `Returned`. If the history stops, ask the seller whether the return has a new number, or see [tracking not updating](guide:tracking-not-updating).

[Peek](/) can notify you of returns as an important step, so you hear when a parcel turns around. It can't stop one.

## How long does a return to sender take?

**The wait.** A refused item usually goes back at once: the Universal Postal Union's rules return refused letters and small packets "forthwith", and USPS returns items it can't carry "immediately". An uncollected parcel waits out its holding period first: 5 working days at An Post, 15 calendar days at Canada Post, 18 days at Royal Mail. [Parcel not collected](guide:uncollected-parcel) lists each carrier's wait and how to get more time.

**The trip back.** No carrier we checked publishes a return transit time. Vinted warns that a returned parcel "may take up to 25 business days to reach the seller".

From abroad it's slower. Under the UPU's rules, a parcel waiting to be collected is held for a fortnight to a month (two months in some countries), then sent back by the lowest-priority route unless the sender pays for air. Expect the way back to take as long as the way out, or longer.

## Quick answers

### I refused the parcel by mistake. Can I get it back?

DHL says a return after a refusal can't be stopped, so ask the shop to send it again once it's back. Canada Post asks you to report a return you think it made by mistake.

### What if there's no return address on it?

In the UK, Royal Mail sends it to its National Returns Centre, which opens it to find the sender's address. If none is found, it may be held for a month in case someone asks, then disposed of.
