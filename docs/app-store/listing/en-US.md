## Description

```text
Where’s my package?

Paste a tracking number, a carrier link, or a whole shipping email, or scan the barcode on the label. Peek figures out which of 3,500+ carriers has the package and shows its journey on a map, every scan, and the day it should arrive.

No account needed. No ads.

WILL I KNOW WHEN IT MOVES?
Sign in with Apple, Google, or a code sent to your email, and Peek notifies you: every scan, the important steps, or delivery day only. An email can tell you it has arrived.

FOLLOWING MORE THAN ONE?
They share one list, the next to arrive on top, riding on its map. With an account, the same list is on your iPhone and on the web at peektracker.com. A widget keeps your next delivery on the Home Screen.

SOMEONE ELSE WAITING FOR IT?
Share the package’s page with a link. They need no app and no account. Wrap it as a gift, and what’s inside stays a surprise until it arrives.

A PASSPORT OF DELIVERIES
With an account, each delivery stamps your passport. Compare passports with friends; your packages stay private.

3,500+ CARRIERS
USPS, UPS, FedEx, DHL, Canada Post, Royal Mail, Evri, DPD, GLS, An Post, Australia Post, La Poste, Swiss Post, PostNL, Correos, Poste Italiane, InPost, China Post, Cainiao, YunExpress, and thousands more: national postal services, couriers, and marketplace shippers.

ALSO IN THE BOX
• A barcode scanner
• Sharing a number or a tracking link from Mail, Messages, or Safari straight into Peek
• Light and dark themes
• English, German, French, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese, and Polish

PRIVATE AND OPEN SOURCE
Peek shows no ads and sells no data. It draws its own maps, so no map service sees where your packages travel. A package followed without an account is forgotten 30 days after it arrives. Download your data or delete your account from the app, whenever you like. Every line of code is on GitHub: github.com/plhery/peek-delivery-tracker

Peek is independent of the carriers it reads. Their names belong to their owners.
```

## What's new

```text
• The Home Screen widget now shows each package as it looks in your list: its card in its carrier’s colors, with its route on the map.
• A package’s page shows the import charges due on delivery and, while they can still be paid online, the lower online price beside them.
• When a carrier refuses a tracking number that fits only one other carrier, the warning names that carrier and offers to switch to it in one tap.
• When a package’s updates look generated, Peek says so, leaves their places off the map and doesn’t notify you about them.
• A delivery postcode can now be in a different country from the carrier’s: the country shows beside the postcode, and you can change it.
• Paste a message that names several tracking numbers when adding a package, and Peek lists them all, in the order written, so you can pick which to track.
• When you add a package, Peek recognizes an Amazon, Shein, Shopee or Temu order number and says where in that store to find the tracking number instead.
• Peek now also reads a tracking number after a French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese or Polish label such as “N° de suivi”. When a postal number’s check digit doesn’t add up, Peek suggests the number you may have meant or asks you to compare it with your shipping message.
• While Peek checks your packages for updates, the refresh arrow turns in place instead of giving way to a spinner.
• On iPad, when the list is beside a package and there’s room, the package’s tracking history sits next to its card rather than under it.
• On iPad, the Passport opens on two pages: your stamps on one, your delivery times and countries on the other.
```
